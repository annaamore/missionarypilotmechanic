Trusting the Flight Instructor Above

The most important part of being a missionary pilot mechanic is the missionary part. If I try and do all the other parts on my own, I will fail every time. Jeremiah 29:11 says “’For I know the plans I have for you,’ declares the Lord, ‘plans to prosper you and not to harm you, plans to give you hope and a future.’” God says this to Israel when they were exiled in Babylon, to remind them that He had not forgotten them, and that He remains in control even when we have to wait. God has reminded me of this many times, especially when I try to take control of my future.

For example, when I was trying to figure out which college to go to, I kept getting rejected for programs or scholarships that I was sure God wanted me to get. “Of course I will win that scholarship, because God wants me to be a missionary pilot and to do that I need money!” But when they chose someone else, I felt like I wasn’t good enough, and started to doubt if missions really was the right path. Opportunities kept being closed off, one after the other, until it felt like there were no more options and I would have to either give up or take out a loan, which in my mind was giving up. At this time I got my ACT score back — and it was just barely good enough to get me a scholarship at Andrews University (and when I say barely I mean it). So that’s why I went to Andrews. We’re still about $20,000 short, but I will be able to earn that much by working part-time, maybe even as a mechanic once I get my ratings done. The bottom line is: God has a plan, and even when I think another way might be better, His ways are best.

Failing the checkride was another time when I had to lean heavily on God. All my hope had gone into passing the checkride and getting my license before leaving for college, and saving money in flight training costs. Once I failed, it seemed like I was going to have to take out a loan, and again, I felt hopeless. But flight classes didn’t even start until the summer of 2027, so I had time to take the checkride while I was in maintenance classes. Even so, I was struggling again with feeling inadequate — I wasn’t a good pilot, I could never become a missionary pilot, and I would never be able to do what I love. I was angry — angry with myself, and turning that anger on to God. “How could You let me fail? I’m doing this because You asked me to, You should have given me the skills I needed!” I think He was reminding me that I really can’t do anything on my own, I have to rely on Him. I also realized this to some degree when it happened, but that just made me more angry. I thought I had been trusting Him, and it hurt to be told I wasn’t. It still hurts a little, but I also learned a lot about accepting disappointment and leaning on Him in the middle of it.
